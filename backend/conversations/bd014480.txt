Here are some things you can try, broken down into three areas: **Preparation, Mindset, and Self-Care.**

### 1. Preparation and Organization (Taking Control)

Stress often comes from feeling like you don't know where to start. Organization is the antidote to overwhelm.

*   **Create a Realistic Study Schedule:** Instead of looking at the whole mountain of material, break it down. Plan specific, manageable study blocks (e.g., 45 minutes of focused work, followed by a 15-minute break).
*   **Prioritize ruthlessly:** Identify the subjects or topics that are most difficult or carry the most weight. Focus your prime study time on those areas first.
*   **Use the Pomodoro Technique:** Work intensely for a set period (like 25 minutes), then take a short break (5 minutes). This keeps your focus sharp and prevents burnout.
*   **Practice, Don't Just Read:** For subjects that require application (math, science, essay writing), spend time actively practicing problems or writing practice essays. This builds confidence and identifies weak spots.
*   **Prepare Your Logistics:** Make sure you have all your materials ready (pens, calculator, notes, water) the night before. Reducing morning stress makes a huge difference.

### 2. Mindset and Anxiety Management (Changing Your Thoughts)

Anxiety thrives on worrying about things you can't control. Shift your focus from the outcome to the process.

*   **Focus on Effort, Not Outcome:** Instead of worrying, "What if I fail?" focus on, "I will dedicate the next hour to mastering this chapter." You can control your effort; you cannot fully control the final grade.
*   **Challenge Negative Thoughts:** When you catch yourself spiraling ("I'm going to fail everything"), challenge that thought. Replace it with a more balanced statement ("I have studied hard, I will do my best, and that is enough for now").
*   **Practice Mindfulness/Breathing:** When panic sets in, stop what you are doing and focus on your breath. Try the 4-7-8 method: Inhale for 4 seconds, hold for 7 seconds, exhale slowly for 8 seconds. This physically calms your nervous system.
*   **Acknowledge the Feeling:** Don't fight the stress entirely. Tell yourself, "I am feeling stressed right now, and that is okay. I can handle this feeling while I focus on my task."

### 3. Self-Care (The Foundation)

Your brain needs fuel and rest to perform well under pressure. Do not sacrifice these things.

*   **Prioritize Sleep:** This is non-negotiable. Pulling all-nighters is counterproductive. Aim for 7-9 hours of quality sleep. Sleep solidifies memory.
*   **Eat Well:** Avoid relying solely on sugar and caffeine. Eat balanced meals that provide steady energy (protein, complex carbs).
*   **Move Your Body:** Even a 15-minute walk around the block can drastically reduce stress hormones. Physical activity helps burn off excess nervous energy.
*   **Schedule Downtime:** Make sure you schedule specific times where you are *not* studying. Watch a show, listen to music, or talk to a friend. This gives your brain a necessary break.
